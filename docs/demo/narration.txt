Software delivery has plenty of dashboards, but understanding a change still means moving between issues, reviews, pipelines and monitoring screens. Clearance brings progress and decision reasons into one shared view.

Every change is a flight. Its author is the pilot, and nine gates form the approach to production. The landing page introduces this language through a scroll-driven journey, with a direct route into the tower whenever you are ready.

Inside the console, the airspace gives an overview and the flight board helps you find a specific change. Search by flight, title or pilot, or filter for traffic that is airborne or holding. The public preview clearly labels its traffic as simulated.

Selecting a flight opens its inspector. Here, the clearance is accompanied by reasons, risk signals and a stage history. The goal is to explain why a decision happened, so a reviewer knows what needs attention.

The world gives that information a consistent setting. A procedural airport, aircraft, city, river and ground traffic support the flight metaphor. Camera presets offer useful viewpoints, while separate expand controls give more space to either the scene or the flight board.

The Governor makes policy something you can explore. Choose a preset, adjust the proposed files, trust or error budget, and watch the decision update. These are what-if inputs; they do not change production policy or a pilot's actual record.

The Trust ledger explains the track record behind a decision. The Carbon view explains the modelled footprint of delivery work and the assumptions behind it.

The interface includes labelled HTML controls, visible keyboard focus, text status labels and reduced-motion support. Responsive panels help preserve the reading experience at narrower sizes. A complete accessibility audit and participant usability study are still planned.

Clearance uses TypeScript, Preact and a custom WebGL2 renderer, with a Node.js server and a deterministic decision engine. The source includes optional GitLab integration, while the hosted experience demonstrates the interface with simulated data.

The result is a working exploration of a simple question: can a richer view of delivery make the next decision easier to understand?
